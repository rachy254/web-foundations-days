# Reflection

The most difficult concept in the course was understanding how systems maintain correctness under concurrency. In small examples, it is easy to assume that a seat, a note, or a record can be updated in isolation, but real systems have multiple users hitting the same data at the same time. Learning about transactions, unique constraints, conditional updates, and the difference between a cache hint and an authoritative state changed how I think about system design. I overcame this by tracing a single race condition step by step, then designing the database operations to prevent it. That made the idea of “single source of truth” much clearer.

The part of my capstone I would improve based on feedback is the way the architecture handles peaks and failure states. The design is strong on the happy path, but it could better show the operational details of queueing, retries, graceful degradation, and alerting during a sudden spike. I would make the system more explicit about rate limiting, idempotent requests, and how background workers recover after failures. That would show a deeper understanding of resilience, not just scalability.

Next, I want to learn more about real-world distributed systems, especially observability, payment integration, and operational reliability. I want to study Kubernetes, monitoring tools, load testing, and fault-tolerance patterns so I can move from designing systems on paper to operating them in production. I also want to keep improving my API and database design skills by working on systems with real concurrency and failure cases.
